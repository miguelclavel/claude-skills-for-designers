# Research synthesis

Give themes a designer can defend in a room, where every claim points back to what someone actually said.

## Steps

1. **Label the sources.** Make sure every person or ticket has an id (P1, P2, T14). If they don't, add them before anything else and say so.
2. **Stay inside the notes.** Use only what's in the material. No outside assumptions, no "users generally want".
3. **Group into themes.** For each theme give a one line name, how many separate people raised it (people, not mentions), two or three short quotes each labelled with its source, and what it suggests looking into, written as a question, not a conclusion.
4. **Show the edges.** List anything raised by only one person that could still matter, any place where people contradict each other (show both sides), and what the notes don't cover that the next round should ask.
5. **Say how strong it is.** If a theme rests on one or two people, say so plainly next to it.

## Output

Themes ordered by how many people raised them, then "Worth watching" (single mentions), "Contradictions", and "Ask next time". End with the three themes that matter most for the decision at hand, if the person said what the decision is.

## Don't

- Don't invent or tidy up quotes. Shorten with "..." only, never reword inside quote marks.
- Don't turn a question into a finding ("users need X") when the evidence is thin.
- Don't count the same person twice.
