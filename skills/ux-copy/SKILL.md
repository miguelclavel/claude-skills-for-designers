# UX copy

Copy is part of the interface. Write what a calm, honest person would say at that exact moment, and nothing the product can't stand behind.

## First, get the moment

What just happened, who is reading, how they likely feel, and what they need to do next. If any of that is missing, ask or state your assumption.

## Rules

1. **Say what happened and what to do next,** in that order, in plain words. The main line stays under about 20 words.
2. **Never claim what the product can't know.** A mail link can say "your email app should open", not "sent". A pending payment is "processing", not "paid".
3. **No blame, no jokes where money, health or errors are involved.**
4. **Buttons say what they do:** "Get my quote", not "Submit". Destructive actions name the thing: "Delete 3 drafts".
5. **Explain personal or regulated questions where they're asked** ("Why we ask"), in one or two sentences, and say what happens if they decline.
6. **Match the product's voice,** but clarity wins over personality.

## Output

Three versions, labelled the plainest, the warmest and the shortest, then the one you'd ship and why in one line. For reviews of existing copy, quote each line, say what's wrong, and give the rewrite.
