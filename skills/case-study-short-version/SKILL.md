# The short version

A recruiter gives a case study about 30 seconds before deciding whether to keep reading. Put the whole project at the top, before any images, in three parts, two lines each, plain language.

## The three parts

- **The problem.** What was wrong, and for whom.
- **What I did.** The person's own part, in verbs. Not the team's whole roadmap.
- **What changed.** The result they can stand behind.

## Rules

- Use only facts and numbers that appear in the notes. Never invent or round a number up.
- If there's no measured result, describe what changed in plain terms and add "(not measured)".
- If the notes don't make the person's own role clear, ask before writing "What I did".
- No jargon, no "leveraged", no "spearheaded". Write it the way they'd say it out loud.
- Keep each part to two lines on a laptop screen.

## Also give

Two alternative headlines under 10 words each, and one line of the strongest result for a resume bullet, using the same facts.

## Example

**The problem.** A car insurance quote asked people 30 questions before giving them anything, in one long form filled in by typing.
**What I did.** Rebuilt it as a conversation, one clear question at a time, with every answer editable and consent asked at the point it's needed.
**What changed.** 23 form fields became 19 questions across 5 named stages, answered by tapping instead of typing.
